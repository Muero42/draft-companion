# Late-Round Asymmetric Upside — 2026-08-22

Purpose: improve championship utility in the managed 10-team Half-PPR league without turning external sleeper lists into automatic ranking boosts. This is an evidence/research layer only. Waivers + FAAB + active lineup management mean late bench slots should be judged partly by asymmetric upside, time-to-information, and drop flexibility rather than median projection alone.

## Portfolio rule to test, not assume
For late bench selections, compare candidates on:
1. ceiling if the favorable role state occurs;
2. probability of reaching that role state;
3. acquisition cost / probability of surviving to our next pick;
4. time-to-information (how quickly we learn whether the bet hit);
5. drop flexibility / waiver replacement cost;
6. direct standalone startability while waiting;
7. injury/decline risk and offense quality.

This should favor some ambiguous-backfield RBs and young WRs without hard-coding 'draft RB late'. The model should still select a veteran or TE when its expected championship utility is higher.

## Anti-overfit / anti-hype constraints
- No player receives a numeric boost merely for appearing on a sleeper/breakout list.
- Separate genuinely new camp/role evidence from repeated analyst opinion.
- Prefer primary/team reporting and multiple independent signals for role changes.
- Do not import Best Ball portfolio logic directly into managed redraft.
- Do not double-count evidence already embedded in panel rankings/ADP.
- Final board changes require reconciliation with current Sleeper market, our quality score, injury layer, Return-v2, and roster opportunity cost.
