# Late-TE Replacement Value — evidence layer 2026-08-22

Purpose: quantify/ground the opportunity cost of early TE without turning the user's Late-TE preference into a hard rule.

## Dalton Kincaid — materially stronger current evidence than a generic sleeper label
Primary Buffalo reporting says Kincaid entered 2026 camp without limitations after recovery, performed strongly in conditioning, and had an early camp practice with two Josh Allen red-zone touchdowns. Buffalo also exercised his fifth-year option and publicly emphasized that prior reduced usage was injury-related. This materially supports health/role recovery relative to stale 2024-25 injury narrative.

NFL.com's current late-round analysis places Kincaid in Round 11+ territory and notes that before his 2025 hamstring injury he was TE6 in fantasy points per game through Week 9; among TEs with 400+ routes since 2023, his 1.8 yards/route trails only Kittle, McBride and Bowers in that cited sample. This is analyst evidence, not an automatic model boost, but it demonstrates a plausible high-tail TE available far later than pick 12.

Implication: the early-TE opportunity-cost model must explicitly include the distribution of later TE replacement outcomes. Passing Bowers is not equivalent to accepting replacement-level TE if Kincaid/Ferguson/other viable later options are realistically available.

## Jake Ferguson — floor/value archetype
NFL.com's 2026 TE value analysis identifies Ferguson around Round 9-10 / TE11-12 despite a 2025 TE5 finish and repeated strong target volume with Dak Prescott. This is a different archetype from Kincaid: less theoretical overall TE1 tail, but potentially high probability of acceptable TE1 production at a much lower acquisition cost than elite TE.

Implication: replacement-value modeling should contain both ceiling-tail and stable-floor late/mid TE paths rather than a single generic TE replacement score.

## Brenton Strange — very-late/undrafted tail
NFL.com highlights strong efficiency after returning from 2025 hip injury and an undrafted market in its cited dataset. Treat as a waiver/late-draft fallback candidate, not proof of reliable TE1 volume.

## Method consequence
For early-TE TAKE/WAIT, estimate:
`marginal early TE gain over distribution of later TE outcomes` versus `marginal RB/WR value lost now + return risk`.
Do not compare Bowers merely to one fixed replacement TE projection.

The later-TE distribution should be conditioned on realistic draft availability and roster timing. A late option's value includes acquisition cost and the ability to pivot on waivers in this managed league, but must be discounted for injury/role uncertainty.

## Anti-overfit
- no Kincaid/Ferguson hard target rule;
- no automatic Bowers fade;
- no assumption all cited late TEs survive to the same pick;
- use Return-v2 / realistic opponent simulation for availability;
- recheck health and market immediately before draft;
- avoid double-counting analyst rank/ADP evidence already present in canonical player quality.

Primary evidence checked: Buffalo Bills camp reports (Aug 3 and current player/camp material). Secondary market/analytics evidence checked: NFL.com 2026 late-round sleepers and TE value/ranking analysis.
