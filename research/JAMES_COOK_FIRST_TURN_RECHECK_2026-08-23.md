# James Cook first-turn recheck — 2026-08-23

Purpose: revisit the user's earlier discomfort with Cook near 1.09 using fresh evidence, without forcing either acceptance or a named-player fade.

## What materially changed after the old Cook skepticism
Cook's 2025 season answered two of the strongest historical objections to paying a first-round price:
- he handled 309 regular-season carries and led the NFL with 1,621 rushing yards;
- he still produced 14 total TDs after the prior 18-TD spike, so elite fantasy output did not require repeating the full 2024 touchdown outlier;
- his 2025 rushing efficiency remained strong rather than collapsing under the heavier workload.

Current fresh expert commentary is correspondingly strong: Andrew Erickson sees additional receiving ceiling if Buffalo expands his three-down use, while Derek Brown treats him as a rock-solid RB1 after the heavy-volume/efficient 2025 season. A current Rotoworld top-200 has him 11th overall/RB5. These are supporting challengers, not independent votes to double-count against PITTI's selected panel (which already contains Erickson/Brown).

## Current role evidence
Buffalo's current camp material shows Cook healthy and working in normal first-team training-camp periods. No new backfield acquisition or health event recovered in this refresh materially threatens his lead role. The main unresolved ceiling question remains passing-game volume rather than whether he can carry a feature rushing workload.

## Real downside that should remain explicit
The 2025 workload was large: 309 regular-season carries and roughly 385 touches including postseason, materially above his earlier NFL workloads. At age 26 this is not an age-cliff profile, but it creates a legitimate workload-regression / durability distribution rather than a free projection of another 300+ highly efficient carries. Buffalo also retains Josh Allen as a major rushing/goal-line force, so Cook's scoring/receiving ceiling is not structurally identical to the highest-volume dual-threat backs.

## Acquisition/timing context
The fresh PITTI freeze has Cook around selected-panel rank 8.3-8.5 and Sleeper Half-PPR ADP about 9.6. The rc4.59 conditional turn study gives only ~11.96% probability he survives from 1.09 to 2.02. That makes Cook a very different sequencing asset from Chase Brown (~97.5% return): if Cook is in the admissible first-turn tier and Brown is also desired, taking Brown first normally burns opportunity value.

## Current conclusion
The evidence does **not** support a generic 'Cook is too risky for 1.09' rule. The stronger formulation is:
- Cook is a defensible first-turn RB1 with proven heavy-volume efficiency and elite offense context;
- his 2025 workload and comparatively modest receiving share keep meaningful downside/ceiling distinctions versus the absolute top backs;
- at 1.09 the decision should remain board-relative (Amon-Ra/JSN/Taylor/value falls, health-adjusted CMC, etc.), not a forced Cook pick;
- if Cook and highly-waitable Brown coexist and their player-quality gap remains material, Cook-first/Brown-second is the correct timing direction.

No production ranking coefficient or named-player rule is changed by this note.
