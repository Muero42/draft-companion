# Brock Bowers 2.02 / 10-team TE economics audit — 2026-08-24

Research-only. No production/runtime promotion. Tier-first remains controlling.

## Why this audit matters
The metadata-safe rc4.59 baseline selected Brock Bowers at pick 12 in **57/60** complete drafts. In those 57 states, Chase Brown was the highest-raw visible RB/WR alternative every time. The Coach raw-score advantage for Bowers was almost invariant: mean +1.110, range +1.097 to +1.117. That stability is a concentration warning: the present TE scarcity/tier machinery is strong enough to make a context-sensitive 10-team decision nearly deterministic.

This does NOT mean Bowers is intrinsically a bad 2.02 candidate. Current expert evidence strongly supports elite-TE status. It means the model's 57/60 concentration requires independent economics validation.

## Independent 2.02 branch evidence already available
The direct multi-candidate counterfactual artifact (current rerun preserves the same mechanism) does not support treating Bowers as an automatic 2.02 winner:
- selected-panel lineup-rank diagnostic: Bowers mean within-state rank ~4.48, better than several alternatives but not dominant;
- independent historical Sleeper market->weekly regular-season bridge: Bowers mean expected wins ~2.49 over 14w across 31 available states, materially behind many RB alternatives and Justin Jefferson;
- within-state utility regret: Bowers mean state rank ~8.68, with only 1/31 state wins.
These results are diagnostics, not policy certification.

## Critical evaluator-bias finding — do NOT use the bridge as an anti-Bowers adjudicator
A new isolated Bowers-vs-Chase-Brown direct-value run on the 57 Bowers states initially appears extreme: bridge-based direct lineup value favors Brown in 57/57, mean Bowers-minus-Brown ~-5.27 weekly points. Component audit shows why this must NOT be treated as intrinsic truth:
- bridge Brock Bowers forecast mean: ~8.55 weekly Half-PPR;
- bridge Chase Brown forecast mean: ~12.60;
- bridge replacement TE: ~4.13;
- bridge replacement RB: only ~1.67.

The replacement construction is a median of the bottom 20% of the full position ADP pool. That is useful as a generic historical outcome lens but is not the correct **10-team actual replacement economics** for an early pick. It makes early RB replacement unrealistically weak and therefore structurally favors RB VOR over elite TE.

Fresh current projections corroborate this concern. FantasyPros 2026 consensus projections (Aug. 23 crawl) imply approximately:
- Bowers: 147.5 standard points + 0.5*96.6 receptions = ~195.8 Half-PPR season points, ~11.52 per 17 games;
- Chase Brown: 214.8 standard points + 0.5*63.8 receptions = ~246.7 Half-PPR season points, ~14.51 per 17 games.
Thus the bridge underprojects Bowers by roughly 3 PPG and Brown by roughly 1.9 PPG versus this current projection source, and—more importantly—its RB replacement baseline is far below a realistic 10-team FA/FLEX alternative.

Current FantasyPros evidence also keeps Bowers firmly elite: consensus TE1/TE2 range; Pat Fitzmaurice's Aug. 19 outlook calls him the Raiders' likely alpha receiver and says he is worth consideration in the back half of Round 2 / early Round 3 of 12-team drafts. Derek Brown's Aug. 21 outlook likewise describes runaway-TE1 upside. This supports candidacy, not deterministic pick-12 priority.

## Correct interpretation
Three facts can coexist:
1. Bowers is an elite TE and a defensible 2.02 candidate.
2. rc4.59's 57/60 Bowers concentration is too deterministic to trust without a 10-team replacement/opportunity-cost check.
3. The current historical market bridge is not calibrated well enough for elite-TE-vs-RB early-pick VOR and must not be used to mechanically demote Bowers.

## Next gate — actual 10-team replacement economics
Before any Bowers/TE production change, compare Bowers against state-realistic RB/WR alternatives using:
- current player projections / independent role evidence;
- **actual or realistic 10-team TE replacement/streaming pool**, not bottom-20%-of-position historical tail;
- actual post-draft FA pool from each branch where available;
- cost of losing the RB/WR alternative at 2.02 and the quality of later RB/WR supply;
- Bowers health/role (currently no acute downgrade) and elite target-share ceiling;
- user Late-TE preference only as a tiebreak/context prior, not a hard penalty.

Do not fit a scalar TE penalty on the current 57 states. Preserve evaluator disagreement. Until this gate is cleared, classify Bowers as **legitimate 2.02 comparison / not automatic**, and prefer superior fallers or clearly stronger intrinsic RB/WR values.
