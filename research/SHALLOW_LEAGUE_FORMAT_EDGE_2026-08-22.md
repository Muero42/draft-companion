# Shallow Managed-League Format Edge — 2026-08-22

League-specific premise: 10 teams, Half-PPR, active waivers/FAAB, 6 bench slots, one QB and one TE starter, 2 FLEX. This format is shallower than the 12-team baseline used by much public advice.

## Fresh external strategy signal
A current FantasyPros shallow-league strategy piece independently argues that low-ceiling/high-floor bench players are less valuable in shallow formats because replacement quality on waivers is higher, and that active bench turnover plus consolidation into elite starters can be valuable. Treat this as strategic evidence, not a direct rule: its suggestion that early QB/TE can be easier to justify in shallow leagues cuts against a simplistic Late-QB/Late-TE dogma and is therefore useful as a falsification check.

This reinforces two project principles simultaneously:
1. late bench should emphasize differentiated upside and churnability rather than generic depth;
2. elite QB/TE must remain available when their actual marginal advantage exceeds the opportunity cost, even though late QB/TE are personal preferences/tiebreakers.

## QB replacement-value implication
NFL.com's current QB value analysis has Justin Herbert around a double-digit-round / QB15 market while arguing for a plausible efficiency rebound behind healthier tackles and Mike McDaniel. Public NFL draft data likewise shows a broad later QB tier. This supports testing, not assuming, that one-QB replacement value remains deep.

Therefore the Coach should compare an early QB's marginal weekly advantage against the realistic later-QB distribution rather than apply a hard Late-QB suppression. Exceptional Allen/Jackson/Daniels/Hurts value can still win when it falls far enough.

## Roster-management implication
Because waivers are active and the league is shallow:
- QB2 and TE2 generally face high opportunity cost unless exceptional contingency/market value exists;
- K/DST benching is especially wasteful and remains excluded from the user's draft plan;
- late RB/WR bets should be judged partly by early role resolution and drop flexibility;
- 2-for-1/tier-up trade potential may make surplus startable depth less valuable than elite difference-makers, but this belongs mainly to in-season trade policy rather than draft scoring;
- replacement-level estimates used by outcome/roster utility should be league-specific rather than inherited from 12-team assumptions.

## New validation question
Audit the independent outcome evaluator's replacement assumptions. If waiver/replacement values are too low for a 10-team/6-bench league, it could falsely reward depth and early positional scarcity. This is a methodological audit only; do not change the evaluator until its assumptions are inspected and a correction is preregistered independently of candidate-policy outcomes.

## Anti-overfit
- do not import FantasyPros recommendations as numeric bonuses;
- do not infer that shallow league = always early QB/TE;
- do not infer that shallow league = pure Best Ball volatility; lineup control means unpredictable spike weeks are less valuable than in Best Ball;
- use format evidence to improve replacement-value and bench-utility modeling, then validate on fresh simulations.

Sources checked: FantasyPros 2026 shallow-league strategy; NFL.com 2026 QB values/current draft data; managed-redraft vs Best Ball format literature as conceptual cross-check.
